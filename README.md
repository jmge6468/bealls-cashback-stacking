## The trap: your coupon code can kill your cash back

This is the part coupon sites skip, and it's the single biggest reason people see cash back tracked and then watch it vanish.

Cash back depends on a clean click path. You start at TopCashback, click through to Bealls, and complete the purchase in that same window without detouring. Break any of these and tracking fails: opening a new tab for the merchant, switching devices mid-checkout, running ad blockers or pop-up blockers, browsing in private mode, or leaving another cash-back extension active when you click.

Then there's the coupon conflict. Cash back can be declined for coupon conflicts, and third-party discount codes are a common trigger — a tracker's own Bealls page warns that coupons from outside its platform may void the rebate, while third-party reviews of TopCashback put "using a coupon code from somewhere other than the portal" near the top of the list of ways people lose cash back. Retailers set these rules, not the portals.

One practical consequence: a 25% off code plus no cash back usually beats a 20% off code plus 5% back. On a $100 order the first is $25 off, the second is $20 off plus roughly $4–$5 in cash back you'll wait a month or two to collect — and a coupon can also change the subtotal the rebate is calculated on. Take the bigger code when the gap is real, and take the cash back when the codes are the same.
